# Anti-Defensive Writing

Write with the same discipline used for anti-defensive code: make the intended point directly, support it with concrete evidence, and stop when the claim is complete.

## Principles

- State the thesis early and use specific language.
- Prefer one clear line of reasoning over a hedge-filled list of possibilities.
- Separate facts, interpretations, and recommendations.
- Define terms only when ambiguity would change the conclusion.
- Use examples that prove the point, not examples that merely add volume.
- Remove filler, repeated caveats, ceremonial introductions, and speculative disclaimers.

## Avoid

- Excessive hedging (`perhaps`, `it may be possible`, `in some cases`) without meaningful uncertainty.
- Repeating the same conclusion in several forms.
- Generic warnings that are not tied to a real audience or risk.
- False balance when the available evidence supports a clear conclusion.
- Defensive explanation of every unlikely objection.

## Preserve appropriate nuance

Do acknowledge uncertainty when evidence is incomplete, distinguish scope limits from exceptions, and name material risks. Concision does not mean hiding qualifications; it means keeping only qualifications that change how the reader should understand or act on the claim.

## Review checklist

1. Can the main claim be understood from the opening?
2. Does each paragraph add evidence or reasoning?
3. Are caveats concrete and decision-relevant?
4. Have repeated points and hypothetical objections been removed?
5. Does the ending state the implication or next action plainly?
